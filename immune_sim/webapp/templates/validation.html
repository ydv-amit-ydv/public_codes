How to read a number like 0.87

Most of the scores above are ROC-AUC. Show the model one real epitope and one non-epitope and ask which is which: the AUC is how often it picks the real one. 0.5 is a coin flip, 1.0 is perfect. It sounds generous because it is: a model can score 0.70 and still be useless for picking a shortlist, which is why the HLA question above is measured a second way — as the share of its top 1% of guesses that turn out to be real. That number is far less forgiving, and it is the one to look at if you intend to actually order peptides.

What was tuned, and what was tested

AMeasured data the model had never seen. It could have failed and did not — or did, and that is recorded.
BMeasured data the model was fitted to. Shows it can fit, not that it generalises.
CPublished directions it was tuned to reproduce. A structured prior, not a prediction.
DAn assumption. Not tested here at all, and listed so nobody mistakes silence for evidence.

A robustness percentage in the table below is not the probability that the biology is true. It is the share of plausible parameter settings that give the same direction. It answers "did I get this by fiddling with the knobs?", not "is this real".

Re-run any of it

The same scripts that produced every number above. They can fail, and a failure is a result: the first out-of-sample run of this model overestimated the human boost response by roughly tenfold, which is how that error was found.
